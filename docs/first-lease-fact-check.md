# First lease guide: fact check sheet

**Status: live since Nov 26, 2026, on the author's approval. The facts below still need a human check.**

Every rule the first-lease guide can show comes from one of the 23 facts below. Each was read off the official page by an AI research pass on Nov 26, 2026. **A person has not yet confirmed them.** Before this guide goes in front of testers as verified, open each source and tick the fact if the page says what the fact says.

How to check: open the link, use the browser's "find on page" for the words in bold, and compare.

Things the research could not confirm, which were left out of the guide on purpose:

- Who owes the rent when roommates sign one lease, and what a co-signer takes on. The state guide chapters that were read do not state it plainly.
- The exact dollar limit on an application screening fee. The state guide gives a figure dated December 2023, which is out of date, so the guide says only that a limit exists and changes each year.
- The 2025 rule about giving screening criteria in writing. The wording read was too compressed to restate safely.
- Whether Long Beach has its own rent control. The city page does not say so; it describes the state law.
- The two PDF editions of the state guide (2025 and 2026) could not be read by the research tool. The facts come from the web-page chapters of the same guide, which do not print an edition year. Worth comparing against the 2026 PDF.

## California Department of Real Estate, Landlords' and Tenants' Rights Guide: When You Have Decided to Rent

https://www.dre.ca.gov/Publications/ResourceGuidebook/gb07_when.html

- [ ] `contract-read`: Both sides should read the rental agreement carefully and understand all of its terms before signing. A renter should not feel rushed into signing.
- [ ] `promises-writing`: The written agreement should contain every promise the landlord or the landlord's agent made, including promised repairs.
- [ ] `basic-rights`: Tenants have basic legal rights that are always present, no matter what the rental agreement states.
- [ ] `deposit-cap`: Beginning July 1, 2024, most landlords can charge a security deposit of no more than one month's rent, whether the unit is furnished or not. _(in effect from 2024-07-01)_
- [ ] `deposit-small`: Small landlords can charge up to two months' rent. A small landlord is an individual, or an LLC owned by individuals, who owns and rents one or two properties with no more than four rental units. _(in effect from 2024-07-01)_
- [ ] `deposit-names`: A security deposit may be called last month's rent, a pet deposit, a key fee or a cleaning fee. Those all count as the security deposit. An application screening fee does not.
- [ ] `deposit-refundable`: A landlord cannot require that a security deposit be nonrefundable.
- [ ] `photos`: For tenancies that begin on or after July 1, 2025, the landlord is required to take photographs of the rental unit. A tenant can ask for copies, or take their own photos before moving their things in. _(in effect from 2025-07-01)_
- [ ] `checklist`: An inventory checklist is not legally required, but it is good practice to complete one with the landlord, and to sign and date it.
- [ ] `cash-receipt`: A tenant should never pay rent in cash without getting a receipt every single time.

## California Department of Real Estate, Landlords' and Tenants' Rights Guide: Before You Agree to Rent

https://www.dre.ca.gov/publications/ResourceGuidebook/gb06_before.html

- [ ] `lease-same`: "Lease" and "rental agreement" mean the same thing. What differs is whether the tenancy is for a fixed term or runs period to period, such as month to month.
- [ ] `fixed-term`: In a fixed-term tenancy the landlord cannot raise the rent during the term unless the agreement allows it. A tenant who moves out early can still owe rent.
- [ ] `copy-15`: The landlord must give the tenant a signed copy of the rental agreement within 15 days of signing.

## California Attorney General: Landlord-Tenant Issues

https://oag.ca.gov/tenants

- [ ] `deposit-21`: Within 21 days of the tenant moving out, the landlord must return the security deposit, minus lawful deductions, and provide an itemized statement.
- [ ] `tpa-12`: California's Tenant Protection Act gives most residential tenants eviction protections after they have lived in their unit for 12 months, and limits how much rent can be raised over a 12-month period. Some homes are excluded.

## California Department of Real Estate, Landlords' and Tenants' Rights Guide: Looking for a Rental Unit

https://www.dre.ca.gov/publications/ResourceGuidebook/gb05_lookingfor.html

- [ ] `screening-fee`: An application screening fee has a legal maximum that is adjusted each year. The landlord must give a receipt that itemizes the cost, and cannot charge the fee when they know or should know there is no vacancy.
- [ ] `holding`: A holding deposit only guarantees that the landlord will not rent the unit to someone else for a stated period of time. A renter should ask whether it goes toward the first month's rent or the security deposit, and get the agreement and a receipt in writing.
- [ ] `immigration`: In most cases a landlord may not ask about a tenant's or applicant's immigration or citizenship status. A landlord may still ask for documents to confirm identity and finances.
- [ ] `vouchers`: Landlords are prohibited from refusing to rent to someone because they receive Section 8 assistance. There is a narrow exception for renting one room in a home the owner lives in.

## California Department of Real Estate, Landlords' and Tenants' Rights Guide: Living in the Rental Unit

https://www.dre.ca.gov/publications/ResourceGuidebook/gb08_living.html

- [ ] `pay-method`: A landlord normally cannot require rent to be paid in cash or by electronic funds transfer only. Other ways to pay, such as a personal check, a money order or a cashier's check, must be allowed.
- [ ] `late-fee`: A late fee that is so high that it amounts to a penalty is not legally valid. A reasonable late fee may be valid.

## City of Long Beach: Tenant and Landlord Resources

https://www.longbeach.gov/lbcd/hn/tenant-landlord-resources/

- [ ] `lb-just-cause`: Long Beach has a Just Cause for Termination of Tenancies Ordinance. It covers tenants who have continuously and lawfully lived in the rental unit for 12 months or more. Some homes are excluded.
- [ ] `lb-help`: The City of Long Beach's tenant page lists the Fair Housing Foundation at 562-989-1206 and Stay Housed LA at 800-694-0040. The City does not provide legal assistance and does not mediate tenant and landlord disputes.
